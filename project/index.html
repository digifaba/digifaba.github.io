The project

Decoding faba bean yield stability

From in-field image data to mechanistic models.

Why faba bean, why drought?

Grain legumes deliver protein with less fertiliser. Faba bean is one of the most promising for temperate climates.

Its weak spot is a dry spell during flowering, and these are becoming more common in Northern Europe.

What we look for

Five signs on the plant. Select a blue marker.

  • 1. Leaf angleEarly observations: short of water, varieties hold their leaves differently.
  • 2. Leaf elongationHow fast leaves lengthen also varies under water deficit.
  • 3. FlowersHow many open, and how many are lost.
  • 4. PodsTheir number and size make the yield.
  • 5. HeightA simple trace of growth through the season.

How it fits together

Experiments sit at the centre. They feed every model, and every model helps interpret them.

1 · Grow

What makes a yield stable?

How
Greenhouse and field trials expose plants to drought, different soils and sowing times. We track leaves, water use, flowers and pods.
Outcome
The traits worth screening for, and data for everyone else.

2 · See

Can a computer spot every leaf, flower and pod?

How
Large vision models are fine-tuned to outline plant parts, despite weeds, clutter and changing light.
Outcome
Open-source tools that read yield components from field images.

3 · Measure

How do you compare two shapes?

How
Instead of a few landmarks, we smoothly warp one whole shape onto another. The warp itself is the measurement.
Outcome
Statistics on whole shapes, capturing leaf angle and elongation.

4 · Follow

Can a growth curve reveal stress?

How
We model each plant's whole growth curve, not just a few summary numbers, and relate it to management and environment.
Outcome
A method to detect stress from individual plants.

5 · Predict

What if next summer is dry?

How
We build a faba bean module, including flower loss, for DAISY, a model of soil, plant and atmosphere.
Outcome
Yield explored under many weather scenarios, to guide breeding.
